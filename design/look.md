# RAVEFORM — Look Bible (v2.5)

Creative direction for the renderer and effects. It complements `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §11 and
`docs/TECH_SPEC.md` §5: those say what exists and how it is built; this says how it should look, with
the numbers that were tuned in the offline look-dev tool (`design/tools/lookdev.py`).

**v2 (2026-11-10)** folds in the spectacle pass that the target frame was already painted with —
colour gradients, soft light, glass, flares, a living background — and adds the composite numbers
(§7), three look variants to choose from (§8), the acceptance scenes the engine is checked against
(§9) and the order in which effects give way on a slow device (§10). Sections 2–5 changed; 7–10 are new.
Where v1 and v2 numbers differ, v2 wins.

**v2.1 (2026-11-11)** is what the first moving pictures from the engine taught. The look was tuned on
a frame of 130 enemies; the endgame holds 700 enemies and 1,500 bullets, and at that density the same
numbers washed the screen out and lost the ship. New: the **light budget** (§3), the ship on the
**priority layer** (§6, §9), an afterglow that is a soft tail on hot light instead of a copy of every
line (§7), and kill, ring and warp-in recipes that hold at a hundred kills a second (§3, §5). Tier
auras are gone. `design/previews/frame_endgame_ref.png` is the second target frame.

**v2.2 (2026-11-11, evening)** is the review of the engine's M1 pictures. Three changes. The
**afterglow follows the light budget** (§3, §7): at endgame density it was a fog worth 0.020 of
luminance, and the frame meets its light targets without it. **Fast shots are drawn as long as they
travel** (§3 line table), so the glow behind them is a tail and not a row of beads. **Drop confetti
arrives as a shower**, not as a frame around the screen (§5). Confirmed as built: enemy bullets are
upright diamonds that never turn.

**v2.3 (2026-11-12)** is the review of the first pictures of the real game (M3 stage A). One change:
the **afterglow is for flashes only** (§7): its knee goes from 0.25 to 0.9. With a single lane of
shots and little else on screen, every shot dragged a long grey smear from the ship to its target
(`captures/m3/bot_0300s.png`, `moto_inapp_touch.png`): at a twentieth of a shot's light the player's

## 1. Principles

1. **Light on black.** The world is made of thin luminous lines. Nothing is filled. Black space is part
   of the composition, at every density: the light budget (§3) is what keeps it when the screen fills.
2. **Hot cores, coloured halos.** A line is a 1.5–2 px white-hot core whose colour lives in its bloom.
   Never make something brighter by making it fatter.
3. **Everything breathes with the music**, a little all the time and a lot on the Drop.
4. **Threat reads first.** Danger has its own colour, its own shape and its own layer.
5. **Shape says who, detail says how dangerous, colour says which side.**
6. **Nothing is one flat colour.** Every hull, shot, spark and ring runs between two colours. It costs
   the renderer one extra colour per line and is most of the difference between a diagram and a light show.
7. **One stroke, one light.** A stroke is lit once along its whole length. Where its segments meet on a
   curve the joint must not show (§4, joints).

## 2. Colour

Palettes are authored in OKLCH and exported as linear-light sRGB (`palettes.json`, field `linear`).
Multiply by the light level from §3 to get the HDR value.

Constants (identical in every palette):

| Role | Colour | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Danger | `#fd3f5c` hot pink-red | Enemy bullets, lasers, lethal trails, telegraphs — and nothing else, ever |
| P1 / P2 / P3 / P4 | `#30ecf5` cyan / `#9bf52c` lime / `#b47efa` violet / `#fdca38` gold | Ship, halo, shots, that player's Shards and UI |
| P1–P4 `deep` | `#4a86fb` / `#1bae54` / `#684ffa` / `#f28f1b` | The far end of that player's hull gradient and trail |
| P1–P4 `light` | `#4ecefc` / `#ddf845` / `#e49ff9` / `#fbea83` | The second colour of that player's halo and Riff rings |
| Pickup | `#fdefba` white-gold | Shards, Boost orbs, Trio orbs |
| White | `#ffffff` | Core lines, flashes, neutral UI |

Per-palette roles: `bg0` (edge of the background), `bg1` (centre glow), `grid` (lattice at rest),
`gridHot` (lattice when stretched — the grid hue pushed almost to white, never a saturated colour, so
a hot lattice cannot be mistaken for an enemy), `accent` (titles, Drop flash, palette-coloured UI, enemy
ornaments), and five `enemyCandidates` of which three are used per run. The selection rule is stored in
the JSON (`selectionRule`): skip candidates within 30° of the danger hue or 25° of a present player's hue.

Enemy hue assignment: the three hues are dealt to families by role — hue 1 to the Track's fodder
families, hue 2 to its hunters and heavies, hue 3 to its specials — so colour hints at behaviour while
silhouette identifies the family.

Every enemy hue has a **partner**: the same colour moved 40° along the wheel, slightly lighter. The
JSON gives both candidates per hue and the rule for choosing one at run start (`partnerRule`: the side
farther from the danger hue and from every present player). Hue and partner are the two ends of
everything that enemy is drawn with.

### Colour fields

A field is a gradient laid over a shape. It is evaluated at the end points of each line and
interpolated along it, so **two colours per line instance** is all the engine needs.

| Thing | Field |
|---|---|
| Enemy hull, shape faces its heading | Along the heading, over the glyph's extent: nose = hue mixed 40% toward white → middle = hue → tail = partner |
| Enemy hull, shape spins | From the centre out, over the glyph's extent: partner → hue → rim = hue mixed 25% toward white |
| Enemy ornament (layer 1) | Flat: 60% palette accent + 40% partner |
| Enemy core mark (layer 2) | White |
| Player hull | From the centre out over 1.3 ship radii: white → player colour → `deep` |
| Player halo, the player's Riff rings | Around the centre, repeating: player, white, player, `light` |
| Player trail | white → player → `deep` along its length, fading to nothing over 100 wu |
| Player shot | Tail = player colour → head = white |
| Spark | Its own colour is a random mix of hue and partner, up to 40% toward white; tail = half of it → head = it mixed 40% toward white |
| Shock ring | Around the centre: white, hue, white, partner |
| Lattice at rest | From the local player out over 870 wu: 55% grid + 45% player colour → grid → 70% grid + 30% third enemy hue. The lattice carries the player's light near the ship |
| Flare arm | Colour at the centre → nothing at the tip (fade exponent 1.6) |

### Soft light

Besides lines the look uses **soft round lights**: Gaussian discs (`e^(−d²/2σ²)`, σ = radius / 2.45,
about 5% left at the radius). They are scene light like any other — they add to the picture and feed
the bloom. Two kinds:

- **Sparkles** — under 16 ref px. Radius in ref px, drawn at full resolution.
- **Pools** — 16 ref px and up. Radius in wu. Low-frequency, so they may be drawn into a
  fractional-resolution buffer; they must never cost full-resolution fill.

| Light | Kind | Radius | Colour | Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Ember | sparkle | 4 px | partner | 1.2 |

At most 24 kill pools are alive at once; when a 25th is needed the oldest fades out early. Small kills
(tier I–II) get the flash only, and theirs is a sparkle: a white pool on every kill is cotton wool at
a hundred kills a second. Only elites and bosses carry an aura. Tier is told by the ornaments — late
in a run every enemy is a high tier, and seven hundred auras are fog. The sparkles on enemy bullets
and Shards are for the High tier and up: there can be two thousand of them, and on lower tiers the
bloom alone carries those lights.

### Light budget

The horde's light is turned down as the horde grows. Without this a full screen is fog: at endgame
density the unscaled look measured 6% dark pixels and three times the luminance of the target frame,
with the ship lost in it (`design/previews/crowd_rules.png`, top).

```
load      = enemies in view + enemy bullets in view / 4
crowdGain = clamp((150 / load)^0.4, 0.4, 1)        followed with a 0.5 s time constant
swarmGain = crowdGain^1.5
```

150 is the target frame's density, so that frame and everything sparser is untouched. At 500 enemies
and 150 bullets the crowd gain is 0.59; at 700 and 1,500 it is 0.45 and the swarm gain 0.31.

| Gain | Applies to (HDR intensity) |
|---|---|
| **Crowd gain** | Enemy hulls, ornaments, core marks, variant marks, streaks and flares — not elites, not bosses. Sparks, embers, shock rings, shatter debris. Kill flashes and kill pools, gravity-well pools and rings. Warp-in rings. Lattice heat above the rest level, and the size of lattice impulses |
| **Swarm gain** | The scene light of enemy bullets, lethal beams and trails and telegraphs (their lines and sparkles); Shards (line and sparkle). There are thousands of these and each needs only its place and colour. **The afterglow** (§7), whatever lit it: in a crowd its source is above the knee almost everywhere and a tail on everything is a fog |
| **Never dimmed** | The player's ship, shots, Riffs, trail and pool. Elites and bosses with their auras. The priority layer (§9). The HUD. The lattice at rest. The background. Boosts and other pickups that are not Shards |

The tone map makes this cheap to the eye: a hull at 0.45 × 2.2 still peaks near 190/255, in purer
colour than at full power, while the light it scatters into the bloom halves. What the player sees is
the horde stepping back and their own ship, the elites and the bullets stepping forward.

Measured on the endgame target (`python design/tools/scenes.py light <png>`): 17% of pixels dark
(brightest channel under 48/255) against 35% in the target frame, mean luminance 0.148 against 0.076.

### Bloom

Six scales from finest to coarsest: Gaussian σ of 1.5, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 48 ref px, weights 0.26, 0.22,
0.18, 0.14, 0.11, 0.09, **total strength 0.95**. There is no threshold: the picture is
`scene + 0.95 × Σ weight × blur(scene)`. Low tiers drop the coarsest scales and renormalise the rest.

### Tone map

```
m     = max(r, g, b)                       // after exposure
tm    = min(1, m * (1 + m / 36) / (1 + m)) // extended Reinhard, white point 6
rgb   = rgb * tm / m                       // hue-preserving
t     = smoothstep(1, 8, m) * 0.7          // very hot values bleed toward white
rgb   = mix(rgb, vec3(tm), t)
```

### Grade (the owner's play, 2026-11-14)

"Color vibrance/contrast pass, game needs a little more color pop, like we did for the Prism powerup."
After the tone map, on the scene only (the threat layer and the HUD are drawn after it and keep their
colours), `look.json` `grade`:

```
Y     = luminance(rgb)
s     = (max - min) / max                  // the colour's own saturation
rgb   = Y + (rgb - Y) * (1.12 + 0.45 * (1 - s))   // saturation and vibrance: the paler, the more it gains
p     = sqrt(Y'); q = p + 0.3 * p (1 - p) (2p - 1) // contrast: an S-curve, 0, a half and 1 stay put
rgb   = rgb * q² / Y'                      // hue and saturation kept; a channel past 1 scales the colour down
```

A pure hue (a shot's core, a line's colour) keeps its place; what gains is the colour the bloom and the
white bleed had paled (halos, pools, the lattice's tint, the background's gradient), and the darks go a
little darker under the light. The M1 acceptance stills (`design/previews`) predate it.

## 4. Shapes

`glyphs.json` holds every shape as line segments in unit space, +x forward, each tagged with a layer:
0 hull, 1 inner ornament, 2 core mark. `size` is the visual radius relative to a standard enemy
(14 wu). `extent` is the farthest point of the shape from its centre in the same units — the length
the colour fields of §2 run over. `facesHeading` says whether the shape turns to face its motion or
spins freely.

- **Silhouette is the family, and never changes with tier.** Hexagon, arrowhead, crescent, kite,
  horned wedge, petal ring, dart, serpent, nested squares, sharp star, gear turret, egg sac, triangle
  node, trefoil, broken rings, figure eight.
- **Tier adds layers and edges**, never a new outline.
- **Variant marks are shared equipment drawn in white:** B (ranged) is twin barrels beside the nose on
  heading shapes (`mark_b_heading`) or three outward spikes on spinning shapes (`mark_b_spin`);
  C (special) is a pair of bracket arcs (`mark_c`). Variant A has no mark.
- **Low-detail LOD:** drop layers 1 and 2.
- **Motion:** spinning families rotate layer 0 slowly (quarter turn per bar) and layer 1 the other way
  at twice the rate; layer 1 snaps one step on each hat. All enemies swell 5–8% on the kick.

**Joints.** Lines are additive, so two round-capped segments that share a point light that point
twice. On a corner that is right — a vector tube's beam dwells there, and hexagons and arrowheads
should have slightly hot corners. On a curve it is wrong: a ring drawn as 72 segments turns into a
string of beads, and the beads crawl when the ring turns. Rule: **where two segments of similar width
meet at a turn of less than 20°, both ends are cut flat** (a one-pixel ramp each, so together they add
up to exactly one line); sharper corners keep round caps. Where several lines cross (a lattice node),
the ones that run straight through are joined this way and the crossing itself stays additive. This
applies to glyph meshes, rings, arcs, trails, lattice lines and text alike.

**Glass.** An elite's hull and a pressed control are filled with a faint flat tint of their own colour
(0.07 for hulls; 0.16–0.45 for controls, §6). It is the only fill in the game and the first thing to
drop (§10).

**Flares.** Tapering star streaks through a centre, for things that should catch the eye without
getting bigger: Blinks (two arms at 45°, 2.2 radii long, partner colour, 0.9, width 1.1), a charging
elite (two arms along its heading, 80 wu, white, 0.9, width 1.2), Boost and Trio orbs.

Traits (elite auras), drawn by the effect system on top of the base glyph:

| Trait | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Armored | Second hull outline at 112% scale, dim |
| Swift | Three short speed streaks behind |
| Giant | Scale × 1.8 |
| Splitting | A crack line through the hull that widens with damage |
| Volatile | Hull flickers toward the danger colour; pulsing core dot |
| Regenerating | Small plus sign orbiting |
| Cloaked | Drawn at 8% between beats, full on the beat flash |
| Shielded | Thick white frontal arc |
| Magnetic | Two horseshoe arcs; stolen Shards orbit it |
| Twin | Thin tether line in the enemy hue |
| Phasing | Every other hull segment hidden off the beat |
| Commander | Three crown ticks and a faint aura ring |

### The player

The ship is a swept dart with a notched tail and a diamond core (`ship`). It is the only two-tone
object in the game (player colour plus a white copy), it always leaves a trail, and it carries a
**halo: one arc per Shield cell** (`halo_1` … `halo_6`). Health is read off the ship, not the HUD.
Losing a cell shatters one arc outward; the last arc pulses in the danger colour.

## 5. Motion and effects

Explosion recipes by size (counts are for the High tier; scale by the governor's density):

| Kill | Sparks | Speed (wu/s) | Life (s) | Extras |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small (tier I–II) | 14–20 | 180–520 | 0.35–0.7 | Sparkle flash; no ring; grid impulse 60 |
| Medium (tier III–IV) | 36–48 | 200–640 | 0.4–0.9 | Flash and pool; two rings to 100 wu in 0.2 s, 60 ms apart; ten slow embers living 1.2 s; grid impulse 140; shake 1.5 px |
| Large (tier V, elite) | 90–120 in a fast thin band and a slow thick band | 150–900 | 0.4–1.2 | Flash and large pool; three rings to 150 wu; ten embers; **shatter** — the enemy's own segments fly apart as tumbling capsules for 0.5 s; grid impulse 300; distortion ring; shake 3 px |
| Boss phase break | 300+ | up to 1200 | up to 1.6 | Clamped white flash; large distortion; shake 6 px; 50 ms hit-stop |

Spark drag 3.2 /s; streak length = speed × 0.035 s, capped at 26 wu. Colours, rings and pools: §2 and §3.

Other signature effects:

- **Shot impact (no kill):** 3–4 sparks back along the shot, 0.15 s.
- **Spawn warp-in:** a ring in the enemy hue contracting to nothing over exactly two beats, then a
  flash on the beat as the enemy becomes solid. The ring starts at 2.5 × the glyph's extent (a Shoal's
  is 30 px across, a Ram's 130); elites and bosses start at 90 wu. A hundred enemies a second arrive
  in the endgame, so the ring is sized to what it announces.
- **Telegraphs (danger colour):** lasers show a thin dashed line for their charge time, then the full
  beam; dashes show chevrons along the path; shooters glint one beat before firing. **A tell is a
  warning, not a threat, so it is see-through** (2026-11-14, the owner: "the red dotted line before
  a laser needs to be transparent"): it is drawn in the scene light only, never on the priority layer
  (no dark rim, no opaque core), its dashes 1.5 px in the danger colour at 0.35, so the lattice,
  enemies and shots under it show through; over the charge's last beat it rises to 0.6, so the moment
  it fires still reads. Every laser's and lethal beam's dashed line, the Longwave lunge's line and
  every other dashed tell follow this.
- **Nova:** on press, sparks appear on a 600 wu ring and fall inward while the screen dims; on the beat
  a thick white front expands to 1400 wu in 0.35 s with 400 radial sparks and a bloom pump (+60% for
  120 ms; +20% in reduce-flashing mode).
- **Dash:** five fading ghost copies of the ship; a Perfect Dash adds a ring and a four-point sparkle.
- **Shield cell lost:** the arc shatters, a panic ring pushes outward, time dips to 85% for 200 ms.
- **Drop confetti:** sparks thrown in from beyond the screen toward the ship: each starts 0–200 wu
  outside the view edge and 0–250 ms after the Drop, at 300–700 wu/s, living 1.0–1.6 s, its streak
  along its own path. It must arrive as a shower. Born on the edge itself and all at once, it draws a
  hatched picture frame around the screen (`captures/m1/strip_bench_endgame_879_sheet.png` of
  2026-11-11).
- **Afterglow:** hot light leaves a short soft tail (§7).

shot can be told from a teammate's; the **head** (the front 65% of the streak; 45% in the first build read as a coloured tip on the

### Beams, lightning and areas (M5, 2026-11-13)

The first pictures of the upgrades drew every beam as a filled bar with a white band down the
middle: Lancer, Accent, the Beam Boost, Bar Line and the Downbeat Cannon, up to 215 px wide. A
filled bar is not a vector picture, it hides what it hits, and Bar Line's eight took a quiet frame
to 39% dark with a fifth of the screen above 200. v2.4 drew the bar's outline instead (two edge
lines and a faint fill between them) and it was still a bar: on the phone the beams read as slabs,
not lasers. The same pictures drew lightning three ways.

**A beam is a laser: one thin line and its glow** (v2.5). Principle 2 holds for a beam as for any
line: a stronger beam is hotter and longer, never fatter. For a beam of damage width *w*:

| Part | Drawn |
|---|---|
| Core | One line on the axis, and it is the only thing in the beam with an edge: 2 px (Accent, Bar Line, the Beam Boost, Standing Wave's lines), 3 px (Lancer at every level), 4 px (Downbeat Cannon). 70% white and 30% the owner's colour at the nose, running to the owner's `light` colour at the far end (principle 6); 1.6 of the shot level at its peak, the Downbeat Cannon 2.0 |
| Glow | A soft strip of the owner's colour, Gaussian across the beam: 0.25 on the axis, σ = w / 5, nothing left at ± w/2. It is a pool in every respect (§3): low-frequency light drawn with the pools, never full-resolution fill. The glow is all that shows how wide the beam hurts, and it is never wider than what hurts |
| Start | Core and glow begin at the ship's nose; the glow comes up over its first 20 wu. Nothing behind the ship |

- **No edge lines, no flat fill, no outline of the beam's width**, at any level and on any tier. A
  body the beam hurts flashes as it does from any hit; that, and the glow, say what it touched.
- **How wide a beam may hurt.** No beam hurts wider than 64 wu, so no glow is wider than 96 px at
  zoom 1. The Downbeat Cannon was 120 wu and comes down to 64; it stays giant by its 4 px core, its
  light, its length and its critical damage (its numbers are looked at again in the balance pass,
  GAME_DESIGN 6.6). Lancer (20 to 44 wu by level), Accent (28), Bar Line (32) and the Beam Boost
  (20) keep what they hurt: a Lancer level that says WIDER widens the glow and the damage, never
  the core.
- **A beam on a beat** (Lancer, Accent, Bar Line, Downbeat Cannon) is at its peak for two frames and
  falls to nothing over the beat envelope's 110 ms (§7); the glow falls with the core.
- **A held beam** (the Beam Boost, the Sweeper's arm, Standing Wave's lines) keeps its glow steady
  and lets the core move between 1.0 and 1.4 on the 1/32 grid. A beam under 12 wu wide is its core
  alone: the Sweeper's arm of part 3, one line, is right as it is.
- **Whose it is.** Core and glow are the owner's colour, like every shot's tail. A Fusion's beam
  (Downbeat Cannon, Standing Wave's lines) has a gold glow and a core of half white, half its
  owner's colour, so the owner still shows.
- **A lethal beam is a laser too.** An enemy's or a boss's beam is danger red on the priority layer
  (§6): its capsule is exactly as wide as it kills (a threat's picture is its hit box), with a
  white-pink line of 2 px down its axis, and round it a glow of the danger colour drawn as the
  players' is (0.20 on the axis, σ = half the kill width) that hurts nobody. (The first number
  here, σ = twice the width, put a red band 180 px wide across the screen behind Event Horizon's
  jets and filled the Blink Star's star: `captures/m5/checkpoint/hz_jets.png`, `bs_fire.png`.) **No lethal beam
  kills wider than 12 wu** (18 px at zoom 1); Kaleidos' beam, 10 wu, is the model. Part 4 drew
  Event Horizon's jets about 90 wu wide and Polyrhythm's burning link and the Blink Star's star as
  bars (`captures/m5/part4/hz_jets.png`, `pr_burn.png`, `bs_fire.png`): the pages gave no width,
  and they come down to 12, 12 and 10. The dashes that warn of a beam are as wide as the beam.
- **Reduce flashing:** a beam on a beat peaks at 1.0, has no white in its core, and its glow is 0.15.
- **Checked on the retaken pictures** (2400×1080, `scenes.py light`): no beam's core wider than
  4 px; `up_barline` at least 50% dark with under 8% of the screen above 200 (v2.3's bars: 39% and
  20%); `fusion_cannon` under 6% above 200 (16%).

### Shots that leave the straight line (v2.5)

Part 3's Wave moved a shot 21 wu sideways in a frame and Helix Array 63 wu (94 px), against 23 wu
forward, on a wave 280 wu long that the stream's volleys, 175 wu apart at 120 BPM, sampled less
than twice. The eye cannot join that into a wave: it sees clusters hopping from side to side
(`captures/m5/part3/up_wave.png`, `up_helix.png`). And above the soft cap the outer Split lanes
fired on alternate steps, so the fan changed shape sixteen times a bar.

**A stream of shots reads as a line.** Whatever bends a player's shots:

- **Beads on a string.** Every shot of a stream follows the same curve, fixed to the aim line and
  measured from the muzzle, so the stream is one curve with shots running along it.
- **Four volleys to a wave, at least.** A curve's wavelength is never less than the distance a shot
  flies in one beat (700 wu at 120 BPM, 483 at 174).
- **Forward first.** No shot moves sideways faster than 0.6 of its forward speed, so its streak
  never points more than 31° off the line it follows. Homing turns (Seeker, the Swarm, Confetti)
  are arcs and already hold this; a bounce or a fork is one corner, not a wobble.
- **A fan is the same on every step.** When shots have to be thinned (GAME_DESIGN 6.6) the fan
  keeps every lane on every step; what gives way is how far the outer lanes reach, and it changes
  on a beat, never between two steps.

**Lightning is one thing.** Arc, Storm Coil, Chain Storm and Tesla Bloom all draw the bolt of
`captures/m5/part3/fusion_tesla.png`: three strands of 1.2 px between the two bodies, each a
polyline of pieces 18 to 30 wu long pushed up to 10 wu sideways, drawn afresh every frame for the
zap's 90 ms; the middle strand white, the outer two the owner's colour (gold for Chain Storm, a
Boost), a sparkle at each end. No straight lines from body to body (part 3's Chain Storm) and no
smooth curves (part 3's Arc).

**An area is drawn by its edge.** A ring, a well, a disc or a field is its edge line and, at most, a
fill added at 0.12: the Black Hole's disc and the Singularity's well were filled to a glow that hid
the bodies being pulled in. The pull is shown by the lattice (it already dimples) and by the sparks
that fall inward.

**What a Boost draws is gold**, the colour of its orb and its card: Colossus' hull, the Carousel's
bombs, the Black Hole's edge, Chain Storm, the blasts of Supernova Rounds. Gold on the field means a
gift that will end. What a Mod or a Riff draws is its owner's colour.

**Time Stretch** has to be seen on the world, not only read on its card: while it runs the lattice's
beat pulse follows the half-time drums (one pulse in two), the vignette closes by 0.15, and enemy
hulls lose a third of their chroma. Ships, shots, Shards and everything on the priority layer keep
their colour: the room slows, the player does not.

Beat response amounts are in `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §11.1; their envelopes are in §7.

## 6. The target frame and the HUD

`design/previews/frame_reef_ref.png` is what milestone M1's stress scene and M3's HUD should resemble.
Lessons from composing it, which are now rules:

1. **Rings are a language.** Shock rings belong to medium and large explosions and last 0.2 s — at
   most six at once. Rings made by a player's Riffs use that player's colours. Interface rings
   (the sticks) are dashed. Three kinds of circle that looked alike made the first draft unreadable.
2. **Keep the lattice quiet.** See the grid rows in §3.
3. **The player must win the eye.** Two-tone hull, halo, trail, the pool of its own light on the
   lattice and the player-coloured Riff effects together make the ship findable in under a second at
   this density. Do not add white effects near it. At endgame density that is not enough: the ship's
   hull and halo are also drawn on the **priority layer** (§9), after tone mapping, with a dark rim —
   the same device that keeps bullets legible. You, and what kills you, are drawn on top of the light,
   never in it.
4. **Colour pools place the action.** A kill leaves a pool of its hue for a moment; at a glance the
   screen says where things are dying and in which colour.
5. **The crowd dims, the player does not.** The light budget (§3) is what makes lesson 3 hold when
   the screen is full.

## 7. Composite and post

The order is fixed: distort → add bloom, afterglow and background → vignette → exposure → tone map →
priority layer → HUD → dither. The priority layer (threats and ships, §9) and the HUD are drawn after
tone mapping and are never bloomed, distorted, vignetted or shaken.

**Background** (never above 0.12 luminance; the scene, not the backdrop, makes the picture):

- A radial gradient from `bg1` at the centre to `bg0` at the edge, both at 0.5: centre at 46% / 52% of
  the screen, radius 0.8 screen heights, falloff exponent 1.5.
- Nebula: soft clouds in the palette's own colours (enemy hues, partners, accent) behind everything.
  About six on screen, radius 350–500 wu, intensity 0.016–0.030, moving at a quarter of the camera's
  speed. The bass envelope swells them by up to 25%.
- Star dust (§3): about 420 on screen, moving at half the camera's speed, three in six white and the
  rest in palette hues. They twinkle ±30% on hats and flare +60% for 80 ms on each snare.
- Low tier: the gradient alone.

**Beat envelopes.** One envelope per drum, the same ones the mix uses (kick: `e^(−t/110 ms)`; snare and
hat: `e^(−t/60 ms)`; bass: the bass bus envelope).

| Driven | Amount |
|---|---|
| Exposure | × (1 + 0.08 × kick) |
| Bloom strength | × (1 + 0.10 × kick); Drop burst + 60% decaying over 400 ms; Nova + 60% for 120 ms |
| Camera zoom | × (1 + 0.005 × kick) |
| Enemy glyph scale | × (1 + 0.065 × kick) |
| Chromatic aberration | + 1.5 ref px × snare |
| Lattice | Kick: a ripple of 7 ref px leaving each player at 550 wu/s; hats: ± 6% brightness shimmer |
| Background | Bass: brightness × (1 + 0.25 × bass), hue drifts toward the accent by up to 8° |

Reduce-flashing mode halves every amount in this table and removes the Drop burst.

**Vignette.** Scene light is multiplied by `1 − 0.18 × smoothstep(0.55, 1.15, r)`, r = distance from
the screen centre with the corners at 1. The Build tightens it to 0.40 over its length.

**Afterglow.** The after-image of a flash — light hangs in the air for a moment where a kill flash, a
Nova front, a beam or a boss break was. Its source is not the picture but one bloom level: the scene
blurred to σ = 6 ref px (one eighth resolution), less a knee of 0.9 on its brightest channel,
`source × max(m − 0.9, 0) / m`. Blurred that far, a shot peaks near 0.7 and a hull near 0.25, so
neither clears the knee (v2.3; it was 0.25 and shots dragged grey smears). A buffer of that size
keeps `max(source, previous × e^(−dt/τ))`,
τ = 110 ms, re-aimed by camera motion, and the composite adds only what the past still holds over the
present: `0.7 × swarmGain × max(previous × e^(−dt/τ) − source, 0)`. A still picture gains nothing.

The swarm gain (§3) is on it since v2.2. Measured in the engine on `bench_endgame`, frame 600: at full
strength the afterglow added 0.020 of luminance (the look-dev model had said 0.0085) and lifted 36% of
the pixels by more than 8 levels, because at that density the blurred source clears the knee nearly
everywhere; the same frame without it met both light targets (16.7% dark, 0.152). At a swarm gain of
0.33 it adds about 0.007. Below 150 of load the gain is 1 and nothing changes.

Fast things are drawn as long as they travel in a frame (§3, player shots): a shot at 1400 wu/s moves
35 px a frame, and a 16 wu capsule read as a row of dashes with gaps. That rule came in with v2.2 to
make the afterglow behind a shot continuous; since v2.3 a shot leaves no afterglow, and the long
streak stays for its own sake. Likewise a Shard flying to a ship faster than 480 wu/s carries a tail
capsule by the shot rule, gold at the Shard fading to nothing, at the Shard's own intensity and
width: a hundred of them homing in are a spray of comets.

The first version kept the lines themselves at half resolution with no knee. At 60 Hz a line that
moves five pixels a frame then prints a row of ghost outlines, and seven hundred enemies each drew a
pillar behind them (`captures/m1/bench_mid_600_signal.png` of 2026-11-11). Line-sharp history is not
to come back: trails that should be sharp are geometry (the ship's trail, shot and spark streaks,
Carom streaks, Dash ghosts).

**Distortion** (displacement of the scene image, in ref px; the lattice bends physically, this bends
everything else a little):

| Source | Shape | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Large kill | Ring moving out with the shock front, Gaussian profile 28 px wide | 10 px fading to 0 over 0.35 s |
| Nova front | Ring, 60 px wide | 22 px |
| Boss phase break | Ring, 90 px wide | 30 px |
| Gravity well | Pull toward the centre, strongest at 60 wu, gone by 200 wu | 14 px |

**Chromatic aberration.** Red and blue are sampled 0.35 × the distortion vector apart, plus a radial
split that grows with the square of the distance from the centre to 0.6 ref px at the corners, plus the
snare kick above.

**Dither.** ± half a code value of noise in the encoded image before the 8-bit write. Dark glows band
badly without it.

**Camera shake.** At most 6 ref px, never the HUD or the priority layer.

## 8. Look variants

**Decided by the user on 2026-11-11: Signal is the game's look.** Laser and Haze stay in the data as
developer switches for comparison; they are not a player setting and nothing is tuned for them.

Three looks built from the same content, made for that choice. They differ only in composite controls, so the engine switches them
while running. Values: `design/look_variants.json`. Picture: `design/previews/look_variants.png`.

| Variant | Intent | Changes from Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Signal** | The reference. Hot cores, coloured halos, a quiet lattice | — |
| **Laser** | Crisp and dark, like a vector monitor in a black room | Lines 12% thinner; bloom 0.8 and weighted toward the tight scales; exposure 1.1; background and pools about 40% down; trails 80 ms |
| **Haze** | Lush and saturated, like light in fog | Bloom 1.3 and weighted toward the wide scales; white point 7.5 and less white bleed, so colour holds longer; background and pools 35% up; trails 170 ms |

Every acceptance picture in §9 is Signal.

## 9. Engine primitives and acceptance scenes

Everything in the previews is made of six things. If the engine draws these six correctly it can
draw the game:

| Primitive | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Capsule** | A line segment with a half-width, two colours (one per end, interpolated along it) and a round or flat cut at each end. Coverage is `clamp(half width + 0.5 − distance to the segment, 0, 1)` in pixels. A flat end has no cap; the coverage is multiplied by `clamp(distance past the cut + 0.5, 0, 1)` instead, so two flat ends that meet add up to one line. Additive |
| **Disc** | A capsule of zero length: a filled circle with the same one-pixel edge. Additive |
| **Soft light** | The Gaussian disc of §3 (sparkle or pool). Additive, feeds the bloom |
| **Triangle** | A flat triangle with a colour per corner, for glass. Additive |
| **Priority capsule** (`thr` in scene files) | A capsule drawn after tone mapping with ordinary alpha: first every dark rim (black, half width + rim), then the cores of ships, then the cores of threats. Colour is display-linear, clamped to 0–1. Threats: enemy bullets, lethal beams and trails (not telegraphs: §5, a tell is see-through). Ships: each player's hull (2.3 px in 55% light + 45% white, 2.0 px rim) and halo (1.6 px in 75% player + 25% white, 1.2 px rim), in addition to their scene light |
| **Background** | The gradient and clouds of §7, added after the bloom, not bloomed |

### Scene files

`python design/tools/scenes.py` writes what the look-dev tool draws as flat lists of those primitives,
and beside each one the picture a correct engine makes from it:

| Scene | File | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| The target frame | `design/scenes/frame_reef.rfscene` (15,264 capsules, 645 soft lights, 208 threat capsules) | `design/previews/frame_reef_ref.png` |
| The calibration card | `design/scenes/look_card.rfscene`, with measuring points in `look_card.probes.json` | `design/previews/look_card_ref.png`, `look_card_nobloom_ref.png` |

Format — text, one primitive per line, fields separated by spaces. Positions are pixels on a
2400×1080 canvas with y down and pixel centres at half-integers. Colours are linear-light RGB already
multiplied by their intensity, so values above 1 are normal:

```
rfscene 1
size 2400 1080
bloom 0.95                       the reference look when the file was written (informative)
exposure 1
bg  er eg eb  cr cg cb  cx cy radius power
neb x y radius  r g b
cap x0 y0 x1 y1 width flags  r g b  [r g b]      flags: 1 = flat at the first point, 2 = flat at the second
disc x y radius  r g b
glow x y radius  r g b
tri x y r g b  x y r g b  x y r g b
thr x0 y0 x1 y1 width rim  r g b
```

A `cap` with one colour is that colour at both ends. Everything but `thr` is additive, so order does
not matter. The background is
`mix(edge, centre, clamp(1 − d, 0, 1)^power)` with `d = length(((x/W − cx) × W/H, y/H − cy)) / radius`,
plus each `neb` as `colour × e^(−dist²/2σ²)`, σ = radius / 2.2. On another screen, scale by
height / 1080 and centre the canvas horizontally.

The picture is
`tonemap((scene + bloom + background) × exposure)`, then the priority layer, then sRGB encoding and dither.

### Checking the engine

Render a scene file with the Signal look, at 2400×1080, with every time-driven effect off (beat
envelopes at zero, no afterglow, distortion, aberration, vignette or shake), capture it, and run:

```
python design/tools/scenes.py compare <engine.png> look_card --nobloom     tone map, lines, colour, layers
python design/tools/scenes.py compare <engine.png> look_card               the same with bloom
python design/tools/scenes.py compare <engine.png> frame_reef              the whole picture
python design/tools/scenes.py compare <engine.png> frame_reef --crop 760,380,440,380    one area at 1:1
```

It prints the mean error, the luminance ratio, how the soft light compares (16-pixel block means,
which ignore anti-aliasing) and, for the card, every measuring point; it writes `<engine>.cmp.png`
with the engine picture, the reference and their difference. Targets, to be reported as numbers and
then judged by eye by the lead:

| Check | Target |
|---|---|
| Card with bloom off: every measuring point | within ±6 code values of the reference |
| Card with bloom off: mean error | under 1.5 / 255 |
| Any scene with bloom: mean luminance | within ±4% of the reference |
| Any scene with bloom: soft-light ratio, 10th to 90th percentile | inside 0.85–1.15 |
| Any scene with bloom: mean error | under 4 / 255 |

The bloom will not match a true Gaussian stack exactly and does not need to; the luminance and
soft-light numbers say whether it has the right amount and reach. A third scene, a **glyph sheet**
drawn by the engine's own instanced glyph path (every glyph in `glyphs.json` at 2.5× and at game size,
with the colour rules of §2), is compared by eye with `glyphs_1.png` and `glyphs_2.png`.

## 10. What gives way first

When a device cannot hold its frame rate the picture loses things in this order, and stops as soon as
the budget is met. Resolution is never on the list.

Never: the priority layer (threats and ships), the light budget, the player's two-tone hull and halo,
hot cores, hull gradients (they are free), dither.

1. Glass.
2. Sparkles on enemy bullets and Shards.
3. Nebula and star-dust twinkle (keep the gradient and static stars).
4. Chromatic aberration.
5. Distortion.
6. Afterglow.
7. Pools: twelve kill pools instead of twenty-four.
8. Bloom scales from the coarse end (six → five → four), weights renormalised.
9. Spark density.
10. Lattice: coarser spacing, flat grid colour instead of the tint field.
11. Ornament layers on small and distant glyphs.
